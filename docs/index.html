Yardstick

By Aakash Mehta, AI implementation engineer · July 2026 · LinkedIn

I built this test and the check it measures, and published the code.

In my test of AI looking up answers in a database, most of its mistakes came with no warning.

If you use AI to answer questions about data, this is how it can mislead you. The check I tested: ask the same question four times (two AI models, each once with a few solved examples and once without). When the four answers disagree, a person looks.

Someone asks a university's database: which year had the most degrees awarded?

The smaller model writes a query that counts rows per year: campuses reporting, not degrees.

It runs with no error and returns 2002. Three years tie on campuses reporting; 2002 comes first.

The right query adds up the degrees: 2004.

Asked how sure it was, the AI said 90%. Nothing on the surface says to check it.

The other three answers all said 2004. They disagree, so the question goes to a person. That is the whole check.

4 Chapter 4

What to do about it

An accuracy score says how often the AI is wrong, not which answers are.

Ask the same question more than one way and compare the rows that come back. When they all agree, move on. When they disagree, have a person look.

  • 87 of 104mistakes caught, reviewing 28% of answers
  • 81right answers flagged for nothing
  • 8silent mistakes got through
  • About a tenth of a centper question at list prices

Limits: one public benchmark, two open Llama models (newer frontier models not tested), never deployed. A two-setup version was not measured.

Appendix

How it was tested, and the raw data

Method

150 questions from Spider, a public text-to-SQL benchmark (50 per difficulty level, 93 databases), each answered by Meta's open Llama 3.1 8B and 3.3 70B, with and without three solved examples: 600 answers. Every query ran against the real database and its rows were compared with the human answer's. The hypotheses and tests (McNemar exact, bootstrap intervals, Benjamini Hochberg) were committed before any data was collected.

Strict scoring

Right information with an extra or missing column counts as wrong. That is 11 of the 84 no-warning mistakes; without them it is 73 of 93 (78%). The quiz leaves these out. Sources for every number: CLAIMS.md. Full write-up: the report.
