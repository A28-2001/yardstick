I built this test and the check it measures, and published the code.

In my test of AI looking up answers in a database, most of its mistakes came with no warning.

If you use AI to answer questions about data, this is how it can mislead you. The check I tested: ask the same question four times (two AI models, each once with a few solved examples and once without). When the four answers disagree, a person looks.

Someone asks a university's database a plain question: which year had the most degrees awarded?

The AI has to turn that into a database query, run it, and report the answer.

The smaller model, given a few solved examples to learn from, writes this query.

Look at COUNT: it counts how many rows each year has, which here means how many campuses reported that year.

The query runs. No error, no warning. It returns 2002.

Three years tie on the number of campuses reporting, and 2002 happens to come out first.

The question asked for degrees, so the right query adds up the degrees column. That gives 2004.

Asked to rate its own confidence, the AI said 90%.

So the wrong answer came with no error and high confidence: nothing on the surface says to check it.

The check asks the same question three more ways: the small model without examples, and the larger model with and without. All three said 2004.

The answers disagree, so the question is flagged for a person. That is the whole check.

4 Chapter 4

What to do about it

An accuracy score tells you how often the AI is wrong. It does not tell you which answers are wrong. For that you need a check that runs on every question.

Ask the same question more than one way and compare the rows that come back. When they all agree, move on. When they disagree, have a person look.

  • 87 of 104mistakes caught, by reviewing 28% of answers
  • 81right answers flagged for nothing along the way
  • 8silent mistakes got through unflagged (another 9 were missed but failed with an error)
  • About a tenth of a centper question for four answers at list prices ($0.17 for all 600)

The honest limits: this was measured on one public benchmark, at one point in time, and never deployed anywhere. A cheaper version with only two setups was not measured. Test it on your own questions before you trust it.

5 Chapter 5

What did not work

Three things I expected to help, and what the data said.

+8 pts

Showing the AI solved examples

No significant gain at any difficulty level. The largest was +8 points on complex questions (p = 0.42). This was the pre-registered question.

0.647

Trusting the AI's own confidence

As a predictor of a right answer it scores 0.647 AUC, where 0.5 is a coin flip. Agreement between the four answers scores 0.861.

78.3%

Sending hard questions to the bigger model

Routing by predicted difficulty reached 78.3% accuracy. Random routing at the same budget averaged 75.5%, and 21.6% of random draws tied or beat it.

Appendix

How it was tested, and the raw data

Method

150 questions from Spider, a public text-to-SQL benchmark (50 each at three difficulty levels, 93 databases). Each was answered by four setups: Meta's open Llama 3.1 8B and Llama 3.3 70B, each prompted with and without three solved examples, for 600 answers. Every query was run against the real database and its rows compared with the human answer's rows.

The hypotheses and statistical tests (McNemar exact, bootstrap confidence intervals, Benjamini Hochberg correction) were committed to version control before any data was collected. Confidence is the number the AI gave when asked how sure it was.

How strictly "wrong" is counted

An answer is wrong when its rows differ from the human answer's rows, even if the right information is there with an extra or missing column. 11 of the 84 no-warning mistakes are this kind. Without them it is 73 of 93 (78%), so the headline holds. The quiz leaves these cases out.

The scorer agrees with the official Spider evaluator on 18 of the 19 queries that tool could score. Every number on this page is listed with its source in CLAIMS.md. Full write-up: the report.
