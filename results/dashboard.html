Every interval crosses zero. A 10-question pilot had suggested few-shot nearly doubled complex-query accuracy (33% → 67%); at n = 50 that collapsed to a non-significant +8. Pre-registration is the only reason this is reported as a null rather than a discovery. Note the honest caveat: at n = 50 the study detects only large effects (MDE ≈ 14–27 pts), so "not significant" is not "proven zero".

Run two cheap models and compare their executed results. Reviewing only the 28% where they disagree catches 84% of all errors at a 16% false-alarm rate. Asking "do two models agree?" beats asking a model "are you sure?" — and costs one extra cheap call.

Difficulty routing failed to beat random

Routing retained 88.7% of the expensive model's accuracy at 28% of its cost — but random routing matched it exactly. Worse, the one-feature length baseline (AUC 0.724) beat the six-feature model (0.665); schema size was pure noise (0.507). Generalisation was weak: leave-one-tier-out fell to chance on complex (0.525), leave-one-database-out held at 0.670.

Two findings worth more than the routing number. The oracle (0.900) beat always-expensive (0.883) at 63% less cost — real headroom exists, the predictor just couldn't reach it. And cost and difficulty are decoupled: question length predicts difficulty (r = 0.55) but not cost, while schema size predicts cost (r = 0.95) and difficulty not at all. Input tokens are 95% of spend because the schema rides along in every prompt.

Deployment recommendation

  1. Spend on the model, not the prompt — and only where it pays. The upgrade bought +28 pts on complex queries and nothing on moderate ones. Few-shot bought nothing anywhere.
  2. Never gate on self-reported confidence. A model claiming 99% certainty was wrong 23% of the time and stayed ~95% confident on its own errors.
  3. Do gate on cross-model disagreement. Two cheap models, compare executed results, route disagreements to review: 28% reviewed, 84% of errors caught.
  4. Assume wrong answers will look right. 82–97% of errors executed cleanly. Put guardrails on results — row-count sanity, join-fanout checks, reconciliation against known aggregates — not on error handling.
  5. Check a length-only baseline before building a difficulty router. Mine lost to it, and both lost to random.

Limitations

Stated plainly, because they bound every claim above.

LimitationEffect on conclusions
Benchmark contaminationSpider is public and predates these models' training cutoffs. Absolute accuracies are likely optimistic; comparative conclusions are more robust since all variants are equally affected.
Underpowered for small effectsMDE at n = 50 is ≈ +14 to +27 pts. A real moderate-sized few-shot effect could exist and go undetected.
Complexity ≈ length ≈ schema sizeHarder tiers came from bigger schemas and longer questions, so the two cannot be fully separated.
Single-instance execution accuracyThe scorer cross-check found one spurious match — two different queries returning the same values by coincidence. Test-suite evaluation would fix this.
Moderate tier is uninformativeAll four variants landed at 82–84%; whatever limits accuracy there is not something this design manipulates.
One model family, list-price costBoth models are Llama variants on one provider; actual spend was $0 on a free tier.
